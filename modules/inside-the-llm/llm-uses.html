What LLMs are used for

7 capabilities · simplest first · how each works

All of these uses come from the same skill: predicting the next token. What changes from one to the next is how much the model has to do with the text, and how much a person needs to check the result. We climb from the simplest uses to the most demanding.

By the end you canexplain how a next-token predictor can do each task, name seven things an LLM does, say which are simple and which are demanding, and explain why the demanding ones need more oversight.

First: how can next-word prediction do all this?

In Parts 3 and 4 we saw that a model only ever does one thing: given the text so far, choose the next token. Sorting emails, translating and reasoning do not sound like that. The answer is that every task can be written as text that needs continuing.

Why does continuing the text give a useful result? Because the model learned from a huge amount of writing, and that writing is full of every task: emails followed by labels, articles followed by summaries, sentences beside their translations, questions followed by answers, worked solutions with each step written out. To predict the next token well on that text, the model had to pick up the pattern behind each one. It does not look those examples up later: what it learned is built into its parameters, the numbers that training adjusted. When your request sets up the same pattern, the most likely continuation is the answer.

How does it know which task?

The model never decides which task to do. Your instruction is part of the text, and it changes which continuation is most likely. Words such as “In one line:”, “Translate to Spanish:” or “Is the customer satisfied?” are cues. The model has learned what usually comes after each of them.

It learned this in two stages. First, pre-training on huge amounts of writing, where cues and their results appear together: “TL;DR” followed by a short version, a sentence followed by its translation, a question followed by an answer. Second, instruction training (Part 3, step 6), where it practises on many requests paired with good responses and people rank its answers. That second stage is why an assistant treats your message as a request to fulfil, not as text to continue.

One thing to be clear about: the model does not search its training examples when you ask. Think of a person who has read thousands of summaries. When you hand them a new report, they do not flip back through old ones. They write a summary, because they have absorbed what a good one looks like. The model works the same way, and that is why it can summarize an article it has never seen.

Last line:

You can also teach the pattern in the prompt itself. Show the model two sample emails with their labels, then a third without one, and it will follow the pattern. That is the one case where it uses examples while it runs, and those examples sit in the context, not in the training data.

Step 1 of 7 · Lower order

1. Sort and extract

The simplest use: read a piece of text, put it in a category, and pull out the key facts. Pick a message.

Already learnedtext of every kind that is sorted or labelled, such as reviews with star ratings, emails filed into folders, and forms with fields. The model has absorbed what categories and key facts look like.

Step 5 of 7 · Middle

5. Create new content

Generation: write something that did not exist before. Text and code are what an LLM produces directly. Images, audio and video come from related generative models, often steered by an LLM that writes the brief.

Already learnedenormous amounts of stories, emails, articles and code. The model has learned how each kind of writing is put together, and it samples from that to produce something new.

Step 6 of 7 · Higher order

6. Reason and analyze

Work through a problem with several steps. The model writes its working one token at a time, and each step becomes context for the next. Reveal the working below.

Already learnedtextbooks, worked examples and forum answers that set out steps in order. The model has learned what step-by-step working looks like. It does not run a calculator: it writes working that resembles the worked solutions it learned from.

Step 7 of 7 · Higher order

7. Plan and act with tools

The most demanding use: given a goal, the model plans, calls tools such as a database or email, reads the results and continues. This is called an agent. Step through one case.

Already learnedcode, documentation and examples of tools being called, plus extra training on using tools. The model has learned what a tool call looks like. Software, not the model, carries out the action.

The pattern

As you climb, the model does more than rephrase: it chooses, calculates and acts. Mistakes also cost more. Checking a summary takes a minute. Checking an action that moved money may mean undoing it. Match the amount of human review to the rung.
