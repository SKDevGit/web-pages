What are tokens?

3 exercises

A model doesn’t read words the way you do. It reads tokens: small chunks of text. Knowing how text becomes tokens explains a surprising amount of model behavior, and it explains the bill.

By the end you cansay what a token is, predict which kinds of text use more tokens, and estimate what a model costs to run at volume.
Step 1 of 3

Split a sentence

Edit the text and watch it split. Common words are usually one token, and a space belongs to the word that follows it. Numbers split into short groups. Rare or unfamiliar words break into pieces.

This page uses a simplified tokenizer that follows the habits of real ones. Real vocabularies differ in detail, and the small number under each token is an illustrative ID.

Step 2 of 3

Compare languages and formats

Tokens per word is not constant. Everyday English uses few tokens. Other scripts, long numbers, and product codes use more. Which of these three do you think needs the most tokens?

Step 3 of 3

Tokens are the meter

Usage is billed and limited in tokens: what you send in, and what the model writes back. A longer prompt and a longer reply both cost more, and the context window, how much text fits at once (your input plus the model’s reply), is also counted in tokens.

The price is a placeholder. Check your provider’s current rates, which usually differ for input and output tokens.
