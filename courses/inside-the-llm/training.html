How training works

6 short exercises

A language model isn’t given rules. It reads a very large amount of text and gradually adjusts itself until its guesses about what comes next get good. Here you’ll run that process yourself, one step at a time, on a model small enough to see.

By the end you candescribe the training loop in five steps, explain what an error score is, and say why repeating the loop makes the model better.

The big picture

This is how a useful assistant is built, in simplified form. The exercises below simulate the pre-training stage, where the model learns to predict the next token. Click any box to see what happens there, and use the switch to see where risks come from and what your business can influence.

Step 1 of 6

Take a text example

Training starts with ordinary text. Take a sentence, hide the next word, and ask the model to guess it. The text provides its own answer key, because the word that really came next is the right answer.

Step 2 of 6

Let the model guess

The model holds a table of numbers called parameters. It uses them to give every word it knows a percentage chance of coming next. Early on those numbers are close to zero, so the guesses are close to random.

Step 3 of 6

Check the answer

Now reveal the real word and measure how surprised the model was. The error score is low when the model gave the right word a high chance, and high when it gave it a low chance. Training is the project of pushing that score down.

Step 4 of 6

Adjust the numbers

The model nudges its parameters so that, next time it sees this context, the right word gets a little more chance. The nudge is small on purpose. Press it a few times and watch the chance climb.

Step 5 of 6

Repeat

One example teaches the model almost nothing. Training is this loop run over and over on different sentences, so the nudges add up. Run it in bulk and watch the error score fall.

Step 6 of 6

From base model to assistant

Next-word training alone gives a base model: it continues any text, but it does not follow instructions. Two more rounds turn it into an assistant. Step through the stages and see how the same request is handled.

What you’ve seen

Take an example, guess, check, adjust, repeat. Large models run the same loop, with far more text and far more parameters. Nothing in it tells the model what a customer or a refund is. Patterns in the text get stronger because they come up again and again.
